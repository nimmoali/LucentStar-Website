Illustrative approach

Why one answer isn’t enough

The questions and evidence to consider when discussing an AI visibility project.

  1. 1One answer

    One check can give the wrong impression.

    Ask an AI assistant about your kind of business and you get one answer: one prompt, one model, one run. It’s easy to screenshot, and easy to read too much into.

    The buyer’s questionWho are good interior designers in Manchester for small office fit-outs?

    1As askedAssistant A · 14 Sep

    Calder & Moss and Atelier North are often recommended. Fernhill Studio is known mainly for homes.

    2RewordedAssistant A · 14 Sep

    For office fit-outs, Calder & Moss and Atelier North are the names that come up.

    3Another assistantAssistant B · 14 Sep

    Fernhill Studio is worth a look for small offices, alongside Atelier North.

    4Two weeks laterAssistant A · 28 Sep

    Atelier North is often recommended. Fernhill Studio mainly designs homes.

  2. 2Different answers

    Ask again, and the answer can change.

    Change the wording, ask another assistant or come back a few weeks later, and the answer can be different. There’s no universal score that settles this across models, and a single number can look more certain than the evidence behind it.

  4. 4The evidence

    Then we examine the evidence.

    An evidence-led review distinguishes what an answer says from what it supports. This fictional example separates three different findings; the scope of any consulting deliverable is agreed for the project.

    Mentioned
    The answer names your business.
    Recommended
    The answer suggests you as a good choice.
    Cited
    The answer points to a source, such as your own website or a directory.
